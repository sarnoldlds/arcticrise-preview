Arctic RISE projects / AVATAR

AVATAR

Automated Vertical Aerosol and Cloud Sampling by a Tethered Balloon for Atmospheric Research

An automated tethered-balloon platform profiling aerosols, clouds and meteorology through the lower atmosphere, aboard Tara Polar Station in the central Arctic Ocean.

Lead
Extreme Environments Research Laboratory (EERL), EPFL, Switzerland
Partners
Tara Polar Station and international research collaborators · UK Met Office
Location
Central Arctic Ocean, aboard Tara Polar Station
Programme
Tara Polar Station first drift mission 2026–2027. Related activities: ICAARUS, CONTRAAST, Micro-Arctic and ARKTIS

Overview

About AVATAR

AVATAR is a compact, quasi-autonomous tethered-balloon platform developed at EPFL. It carries scientific instruments through the lower atmosphere to provide high-resolution vertical profiles of aerosol and cloud properties, together with key meteorological variables.

Measurements taken at the surface do not necessarily represent conditions higher up. Temperature inversions, which prevail in polar regions, can strongly influence the vertical distribution of aerosol particles and the formation and properties of clouds. Understanding these processes requires measurements throughout the atmospheric column.

Designed to be operated by a single person, AVATAR can be deployed from remote field sites, sea ice and research vessels, making it well suited to long-term, quasi-automated observations in polar environments.

The platform is being deployed as part of the atmospheric research programme aboard Tara Polar Station, a new polar research vessel designed to drift with the Arctic sea ice. During the mission, AVATAR will perform regular profiles to investigate the seasonal evolution of the lower Arctic atmosphere and its interactions with aerosols and clouds.

Approach

How it works

AVATAR combines three main components, designed to be compact, robust and transportable for deployment in demanding conditions, including from sea ice and the deck of a moving research vessel.

  • The instrument payload

    A lightweight, insulated and protected enclosure houses instruments that continuously measure aerosol particle concentrations and size distributions, cloud droplets, wind, temperature, humidity and pressure. A forward-facing camera documents conditions during each flight.

  • The tethered balloon and winch

    The payload hangs beneath a Helikite, a hybrid helium balloon and kite. A custom-built electric winch holding up to 2,000 m of tether controls ascent and descent, either manually or through an automated flight-control system.

  • The ground control station

    A laptop running dedicated software receives the measurements in real time by radio telemetry, displays the incoming data, and lets the operator control the winch and manage the profiling sequence.

Objectives

What AVATAR will do

  1. Characterise the vertical structure of the lower atmosphere. Regular profiles of temperature, relative humidity, wind and pressure to investigate stratification, boundary-layer dynamics and temperature inversions.
  2. Investigate aerosol and cloud properties. Measure aerosol number concentrations and size distributions, and cloud droplet size distributions, from near the surface to higher atmospheric layers.
  3. Understand aerosol–cloud interactions. Investigate how aerosol particles are distributed within and around clouds, how they contribute to cloud formation, and how these processes evolve under different atmospheric conditions.
  4. Explore the seasonal evolution of the Arctic atmosphere. Profile regularly during the Tara Polar Station drift to document changes in aerosol and cloud properties through the Arctic seasonal cycle.
  5. Advance autonomous atmospheric observations. Demonstrate the potential of a lightweight, quasi-autonomous tethered-balloon system for sustained profiling in remote and logistically challenging environments.

Outcomes

Expected outcomes

During the project

  • Regular vertical profiles over the central Arctic Ocean of meteorological variables, aerosol particles and cloud droplets.
  • Delivery of data to the UK Met Office to evaluate their potential for assimilation in numerical weather prediction (NWP).
  • New observations of the vertical distribution of aerosol particles and their relationship with cloud properties.
  • Improved understanding of the thermodynamic structure of the Arctic lower atmosphere, including temperature inversions and boundary-layer processes.
  • A unique dataset documenting atmospheric properties through the seasonal cycle of the Tara Polar Station drift.
  • Demonstration of an automated tethered-balloon platform for atmospheric research in polar environments.

Longer term

  • New constraints for atmospheric process studies, climate modelling and operational NWP.
  • A transferable, scalable approach to autonomous vertical atmospheric observations in remote environments.
  • Scientific publications and open research datasets contributing to international polar atmospheric research.

Arctic RISE

Part of a wider effort

AVATAR contributes to the broader effort to improve routine atmospheric observations in the Arctic, where the scarcity of vertical measurements remains a major limitation for understanding atmospheric processes and evaluating weather and climate models.

By measuring through the lower atmospheric column, AVATAR complements surface-based observations and remote sensing. Its measurements of aerosol and cloud properties at different altitudes are particularly valuable for understanding Arctic cloud formation and its influence on the regional energy budget.

Through the atmospheric research programme of Tara Polar Station, AVATAR also supports sustained, collaborative and innovative approaches to polar atmospheric observation, and to the evaluation and development of model processes.
